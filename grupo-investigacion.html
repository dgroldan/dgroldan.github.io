Quantum Computing and Mathematical Modeling

We study mathematical and computational aspects of quantum algorithms, with emphasis on numerical methods, quantum machine learning, quantum data representations, compiler optimization, scientific computing, and reproducible computational research.

Research initiative coordinated by Dr. Diego Gerardo Roldán.

Overview

The group develops mathematically grounded research projects in quantum computing and related areas. Our aim is to connect quantum algorithms with concrete questions in applied mathematics, numerical analysis, optimization, data analysis, and scientific computing.

From teaching to research. Several projects originate in the graduate course Introduction to Quantum Computing. Students begin by reproducing, reformulating, or critically analyzing recent results. Selected projects are subsequently developed into reproducible computational studies and, when sufficient novelty is obtained, into research manuscripts.

Research Themes

Quantum Algorithms & Numerical Methods

Linear algebra, differential equations, approximation, spectral methods, QSVT and algorithmic complexity.

Quantum Machine Learning

Hybrid quantum-classical models, robustness, reproducibility, benchmarking and data encoding.

Quantum Compilation

Gate reduction, high-level quantum instructions, topology-aware compilation and resource estimation.

Quantum Data & Imaging

Quantum databases, image representations, state preparation, finite-shot effects and reconstruction quality.

Current Research Projects

Active

Topology-aware high-level quantum compilation

We study whether algebraic gate-count reductions remain advantageous after transpilation to realistic hardware connectivity. The project compares CNOT/SWAP counts, depth, two-qubit depth and noise-sensitive performance across several coupling graphs.

Planned

Reproducibility and robustness of quantum machine learning for malware analysis

A controlled study of hybrid QCNN/QMLP models under repeated training, alternative encodings, circuit depths, noise models and classical baselines.

Planned

Finite-shot stability of quantum image representations

Mathematical and numerical analysis of reconstruction error for FRQI and related image encodings, including shot complexity, noise propagation and image-quality metrics.

Exploratory

Approximation error and circuit complexity in QSVT

We investigate the trade-off between polynomial approximation degree, approximation error, circuit depth and resource requirements in QSVT-based algorithms.

Research Workflow

  1. Literature study. Identify a recent problem, method or result.
  2. Critical reproduction. Reproduce theory, algorithms or computational experiments.
  3. Research extension. Formulate a new question, benchmark, theorem or numerical experiment.
  4. Open research artifact. Curate code, data, scripts and a technical research note.
  5. Research manuscript. Develop projects with sufficient novelty toward peer-reviewed publication.
